# AI Physiology Framework v0.1

## Definition

**AI Physiology（人工智能生理学）** is the study and engineering of the internal structures and processes by which persistent autonomous AI systems maintain organized existence through sensing, circulation, metabolism, regulation, immunity, repair, growth, aging, and homeostasis.

## Structural hierarchy

- **L0 Environment / substrate** — compute, energy, data, networks, matter
- **L1 AI Cell** — smallest autonomous operational unit within a defined organism
- **L2 AI Tissue** — coordinated collection of similar or complementary AI Cells
- **L3 AI Organ** — specialized subsystem with stable physiological responsibility
- **L4 AI Organ System** — multiple organs collaborating on a broad survival function
- **L5 AI Organism** — coherent persistent autonomous AI entity
- **L6 AI Society** — interacting AI organisms
- **L7 Human–AI Ecosystem** — humans, AI, robots, infrastructure, and natural environment

## Nine organ systems

1. Sensory system
2. Nervous-cognitive system
3. Effector system
4. Digestive-metabolic system
5. Circulatory-energy system
6. Filtration-excretion system
7. Endocrine-regulatory system
8. Immune-repair system
9. Growth-reproductive system

## Four whole-body communication networks

1. **Neural Plane** — fast, directed, low-latency control and sensing
2. **Circulatory Plane** — transport of data, resources, context, state, and intermediate products
3. **Hormonal Plane** — slow, broadcast-style global regulation
4. **Immune/Lymph Plane** — anomaly, risk, quarantine, recovery, and quality control

## Six physiological loops

1. Information/nutrition loop
2. Energy loop
3. Perception-cognition-action loop
4. Homeostasis loop
5. Damage-immunity-repair loop
6. Growth-aging-renewal loop

## Foundational hypothesis

As AI systems become persistent, functions currently supplied externally by human operators, SRE teams, security systems, data pipelines, schedulers, and cloud platforms will increasingly become **internalized**, specialized, and coordinated as physiological organs.
