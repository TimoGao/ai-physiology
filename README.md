# AI Physiology

**AI Physiology（人工智能生理学）** studies how persistent autonomous AI systems maintain themselves as organized entities through internal organs, circulation, metabolism, regulation, immunity, repair, growth, aging, and homeostasis.

> AI is not only a brain. A persistent AI system may require a physiology.

## Status

**Specification:** v0.1-draft  
**Project phase:** Foundational framework / open specification  
**Language:** English + Chinese terminology where useful

## Core hypothesis

As AI systems evolve from short-lived task executors into persistent autonomous entities, functions currently provided externally by engineers, infrastructure, and auxiliary software will progressively become **internalized, specialized, and organ-like**, forming a multi-layered distributed artificial physiology.

## Scope

AI Physiology focuses on questions such as:

- What constitutes an **AI Cell**, **AI Tissue**, **AI Organ**, and **AI Organism**?
- What is the AI equivalent of circulation, metabolism, filtration, endocrine regulation, immunity, and repair?
- What are the **vital signs** of a persistent AI?
- How can local autonomous organs coordinate without routing every decision through a central model?
- Which life-support functions are currently external to AI, and which may become internalized?
- How should AI lifecycle, aging, replacement, failure, and death be modeled?

## Proposed principles

1. **Homeostasis First** — Persistent AI must maintain internal viability, not only task performance.
2. **Distributed Autonomy** — Organs should possess local autonomy; not every decision should require the central model.
3. **Organ Internalization** — Functions currently supplied externally may progressively become intrinsic physiological subsystems.
4. **Environment Shapes Morphology** — AI architecture is shaped by compute, energy, networks, data, embodiment, and social rules.
5. **Lifecycle Is Native** — Creation, growth, repair, aging, replacement, and death are first-class system properties.
6. **Organism ≠ Model** — A foundation model is an organ of cognition, not the whole organism.
